Verizon's channel chief bolts for a health insurer, and partners are already nervous

Channel Dive · August 10, 2026
Mark Tina, a 23-year Verizon veteran and VP of Indirect Partner Sales, is leaving to run national partner sales and distribution at Humana, in a newly created role that has nothing to do with telecom. Tina built Verizon's indirect channel from what he called "only single-digit indirect distribution" into "healthy double digits," running a 700-person org that reported to Verizon Business CEO Kyle Malady. John Constantino, Verizon's VP of Business Sales, inherits the indirect book on top of his existing job. No word yet on whether indirect gets a dedicated leader again or stays folded under Constantino permanently. Partners aren't hiding their nerves: Connected Solutions Group CEO Michael Pittman called Tina "a champion for the programs" and "one of the better human beings I have experienced in business."
Dale's read

Losing the executive who built your indirect motion from scratch is bad enough; losing them to an industry with nothing to do with telecom means this wasn't about a better comp plan down the street. Any TSD running on personal relationships instead of program mechanics should be nervous. If Constantino treats indirect as a side project, expect slower answers out of Verizon's channel org for a quarter or two. Get a second contact on file now.

Fake IDs made by AI are now a five-dollar problem, and search interest is up 3,800 percent

CX Today · August 24, 2026
Global search interest in "AI identity fraud" is up roughly 3,826 percent over the past year to around 14,000 monthly searches, with a 24 percent jump in just the last three months. The driver is access, not sophistication. Ben O'Brien of ID Card Centre put it plainly: forging documents "used to require skill; now it just requires access to a generator." That covers both AI-faked government IDs and synthetic identities blending real stolen data with fabricated details, and both are built to slide past the quick visual check a front-line employee actually has time to run. Hospitality, retail, recruitment, healthcare, real estate and education carry the most exposure, anywhere a remote interaction or a rushed decision stands in for real verification.
Dale's read

This isn't a contact-center trend piece, it's a prospecting hook. Every business that verifies identity over a call or a chat now has a documented, quantified reason to fix fraud controls this quarter instead of next. Lead with "3,800 percent," not "synthetic identity." The number gets a meeting, the jargon gets a blank stare. Anyone still selling voice or contact center on features alone is leaving an easy urgency argument sitting on the table.

France's SFR gets hit, customer data walks out through a fiber management tool

Light Reading · August 21, 2026
French carrier SFR confirmed a breach after attackers exploited vulnerabilities in a tool used to manage fiber connections, exposing customer postal addresses, emails and phone numbers. SFR hasn't said how many subscribers are affected, but says it suspended access to the affected systems immediately and has since added new security measures.
Dale's read

Another carrier learns that provisioning and back-office tools are as much an attack surface as the network itself. Worth raising with any prospect who assumes their provider's plumbing isn't their problem.

OpenAI tries to make enterprises comfortable handing over sensitive data

CX Today · August 20, 2026
OpenAI unveiled Private Safety Processing, built on zero data retention so prompts and responses aren't kept after a request completes. The pitch targets enterprises sitting on financial records, health data or confidential business plans who've held off adopting AI tools over exactly that exposure. Automated risk detection runs without giving OpenAI staff access to retained content, with a choice between enterprise-controlled and encrypted customer-key infrastructure. Rollout starts in September.
Dale's read

Every AI vendor bolted onto a sales or CX tool eventually gets the "where does my data go" question. OpenAI just published the answer it wants copied. Expect UCaaS and CCaaS vendors to borrow this language fast. If a vendor can't answer as cleanly, ask them why.

Data center spending is on pace to blow past three trillion dollars by 2030

Light Reading · August 18, 2026
Dell'Oro Group now forecasts worldwide data center capex surpassing $3 trillion by 2030, with high-end AI accelerators claiming the largest share. A newly tracked segment, AI-specialized cloud providers and model builders, is growing at nearly a 60 percent compound annual rate, well ahead of every other customer category. General-purpose servers get pulled along too, as inference and agentic workloads add demand beyond pure model training.
Dale's read

The number is big enough to be numbing, so use it differently: it funds every AI feature about to land in your CRM, dialer and contact center platform, whether the prospect asked for it or not. This capex boom is upstream of the pitch you'll be giving in six months.
