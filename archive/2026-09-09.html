OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra Clears Its Own Cyber Threshold, and the Internet Noticed

UC Today · Sep 2, 2026
OpenAI shipped GPT-6 Astra, the first model to cross the company's own "Critical" cybersecurity threshold under its Preparedness Framework. Astra scored 100% on ExploitBench, found two previously unknown vulnerabilities during evaluation, and built a browser compromise chain that escaped a sandbox and ran commands on the host machine, chaining discovery through exploit development with far less human steering than earlier models needed. OpenAI says it paused development mid cycle to rebuild safeguards before shipping: chain of thought monitoring, and a 91.5% refusal rate on disallowed requests versus 59% for the prior model, with zero infrastructure compromise attempts in honeypot testing against a 56% rate before. Broad access stays limited to a small testing group rather than a public rollout, a deliberate throttle on how fast this particular capability spreads.
Dale's read

A model that can chain vulnerability discovery to a working exploit with minimal hand holding isn't an abstract research milestone, it's the tool set behind the next wave of automated attacks on business phone systems, SIP trunks, and the contact center stack that already gets probed for toll fraud. Bring this up before a prospect does: ask what their current vendor's answer is to AI accelerated reconnaissance, not just AI powered support.

Cisco's Router and Switch Bug Haul Lands on Carrier-Grade Gear

The Register · Sep 4, 2026
Cisco disclosed at least six critical and high severity flaws across IOS XR, the operating system behind carrier-grade routing and switching gear, plus ten Nexus 9000 switch models. Two IOS XR bugs rate 9.8 out of 10 on CVSS: an out of bounds write and a missing authentication check on a critical function. The Nexus flaw is worse in practice: TCP ports 43210 and 43211 sit open by default and let an unauthenticated remote attacker run code with root privileges, and Cisco has no permanent fix yet, only an access control list workaround. No exploitation in the wild has been reported. IOS XR customers have patched builds to install now; Nexus 9000 owners need to lock down those two ports today, not after the next maintenance window.
Dale's read

This sits in the infrastructure layer under every business phone and contact center platform that rides a customer's own network, so it's fair game in any security conversation this quarter. A prospect running Nexus 9000 gear with those ports wide open has a problem regardless of which UCaaS platform they pick, and flagging it costs nothing and buys real credibility. No confirmed exploitation yet isn't the same as no urgency.

Zoom Phone Is Quietly Zoom's Best Salesperson

No Jitter · Aug 26, 2026
Zoom's fiscal Q2 2027 revenue hit $1.28 billion, up 4.9% year over year, with enterprise revenue growing 7.8%, its strongest pace in three years. CFO Michelle Chang credited Zoom Phone as a pull through product: five of the top ten Phone deals and three of the top ten Contact Center deals overlapped. AI feature usage grew 125% year over year.
Dale's read

Zoom isn't winning enterprise deals on phone features anymore, it's winning them by using Phone as the wedge into contact center and AI seats. That's the bundle motion to watch and counter, not the dial tone.

FCC Pays Off Its Rip and Replace Loan Early, Off the Back of a Spectrum Auction

Broadband Breakfast · Sep 8, 2026
The FCC repaid the $3.08 billion it borrowed from the Treasury in March 2025 to fund the program that reimburses carriers for ripping out Huawei and ZTE gear. Congress had appropriated only about 39.5% of approved costs. June's Auction 113 for AWS-3 spectrum raised $3.572 billion, covering the loan with room to spare.
Dale's read

The program is finally funded through completion, which matters most to the small and rural carriers still waiting on reimbursement checks. It also confirms spectrum auctions are back as a real revenue lever, worth watching for what gets freed up next.
