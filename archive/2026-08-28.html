Salesforce Turns Its Own CRM Into a Claude Plugin

No Jitter · Aug 28, 2026
Salesforce posted $11.3 billion in Q2 revenue, up 11 percent, and used the earnings call to launch Claudeforce, a deepened tie-up with Anthropic. The centerpiece is "Salesforce in Claude," a plugin letting sellers run CRM workflows and 37 prebuilt sales skills without opening Salesforce's own interface, routed through AIforce, its connector layer for outside models. Pilot customers have it now; open beta lands in September. No pricing has been announced. Benioff called it pairing "Claude's extraordinary intelligence and reasoning with Salesforce's trusted data," which also reads as an admission that reps have been avoiding the actual Salesforce UI for years. It's the first time Salesforce has put the "force" suffix on somebody else's product.
Dale's read

If your team already lives in chat instead of a CRM tab, Claudeforce is the vendor admitting the click-heavy interface was the real problem, not adoption. For a pipeline review, that's a data point: rivals' reps get faster forecast updates and next-best-action prompts without touching a dashboard. Watch what this does to CPQ integrations industry-wide. If Claude becomes the front door to CRM data, every UCaaS sales stack bolting on AI coaching needs a plugin story too, not just another dashboard.

AI Is Eating the Referral, So One TSD Built a Database to Fight Back

Channel Dive · Aug 25-26, 2026
A new AI Revenue Institute survey of 521 B2B buyers finds over half have dropped a vendor after an AI tool flagged a shortcoming, and 82 percent say they'd get more skeptical of whoever referred that vendor in the first place. Cloud Communications Group's Tyler Dool put it plainly: "now you have to go into those same conversations knowing that a second opinion is extremely easy to get." CCG's answer, launching in September, is InsightForge, a free decision-intelligence platform built on 19 years of its own consulting data, meant to give purchasing committees visibility a chatbot can't fake. Other advisors caution frontier models still lack real vendor data and can hallucinate, which is the whole argument for the tool existing.
Dale's read

This is the channel model's actual exposure, and it isn't AI selling for the prospect, it's AI second-guessing the human who referred you. A TSD or agent relationship used to be worth something because the buyer trusted the recommender's judgment; now that judgment gets fact-checked live by a free chatbot. CCG's bet is proprietary data beats a general model's guesswork. If you sell through advisors, ask what data they actually hold that a chatbot doesn't, because "relationship" alone isn't the moat it was two years ago.

Real-Time AI Coaching Is One Setting Away From Surveillance

No Jitter · Aug 28, 2026
IDC finds 24 percent of organizations rank AI-enabled coaching platforms among their top three AI priorities for the next 18 months, the same tools that score tone, sentiment, and script adherence on live calls. IDC's Amy Loomis says the line between coaching and surveillance is intent, not the technology. Strike Graph's Justin Beals frames the fix as transparency: if the rep sees the same score the manager sees, it's coaching, not monitoring.
Dale's read

Rolling this out on your own team without disclosure is the fastest way to torch morale over a tool that was supposed to help. If you're piloting call-scoring AI, put the dashboard in front of reps first, not last, and don't let a probabilistic sentiment score decide a review by itself.

Lightyear Ships an Agent That Actually Runs the RFP

RCR Wireless · Aug 25, 2026
Lightyear's new Dispatch platform routes natural-language requests to specialized agents: one sources and normalizes carrier bids in hours instead of the usual weeks of RFP cycling, another tracks multi-site, multi-carrier installs and auto-escalates delays. It's live now for existing customers, with renewals and invoice disputes on the 2027 roadmap. No pricing disclosed.
Dale's read

An agent that compresses carrier quoting from weeks to hours is a direct hit on the "give me a week and I'll get you options" line half this beat still runs. Enterprise buyers comparing bids faster means less room to control the timeline on your terms, not more.

HP Folds Poly's Headsets Into Its IT Management Platform

UC Today · Aug 27, 2026
HP posted record Q3 revenue of $15.7 billion, up 12.5 percent, even as PC shipments fell 16 percent. The growth came from "attached" businesses, peripherals, collaboration gear, and services, now a third of division gross profit. Poly Lens, the cloud management layer for headsets and video endpoints, is built into HP's Workforce Experience Platform, putting collaboration devices under the same dashboard as laptops and printers.
Dale's read

HP is betting IT buys collaboration hardware where it already manages endpoints, which narrows the lane for anyone pitching UC gear as its own decision. If your pitch depends on a standalone device conversation, expect more IT buyers who already treat headsets as a line item on the laptop refresh, not their own RFP.

Barracuda Admits Its Partners Aren't Cleanly MSP or Reseller Anymore

Channel Dive · Aug 27, 2026
Barracuda merged its separate MSP and reseller partner tiers into one Partner Success Program after conceding that a growing share of partners run both models at once and were only getting revenue credit for the resale half. Channels SVP Michelle Hodges: "We cannot dictate how the customer needs to consume their technology, or how the partner delivers that technology."
Dale's read

Comp models built around a clean MSP-versus-reseller line are already out of date; most partners on this beat blend transactional and managed revenue and get shortchanged by programs that only count one. If your own partner comp still forces that binary choice, this is a preview of the complaint you'll hear next.

Every State's BEAD Plan Is Finally Approved, $18 Billion Now Moves

Broadband Breakfast · Aug 28, 2026
Illinois's approval this week closed out all 56 states and territories, clearing $18.19 billion in broadband deployment spending across 3.79 million locations: 63 percent fiber, 22 percent satellite, 13 percent fixed wireless. A separate $22.5 billion in non-deployment funding is still waiting on NTIA guidance on how it can be used.
Dale's read

Fiber-funded builds mean new last-mile competition showing up in markets that didn't have a credible alternative to the incumbent cable or telco. Track which of your territories sit inside a BEAD-funded footprint, because a subsidized new entrant changes the bundle conversation whether or not the build finishes on schedule.
