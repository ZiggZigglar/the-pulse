Epiris Takes Gamma Communications Private in a £1.08 Billion Deal

UC Today · Sep 1, 2026
Private equity firm Epiris will take Gamma Communications private in a £1.08 billion deal, paying 1,120p per share in cash, a 53% premium over the 732p close before bidding started in May. Gamma powers the O2 Daisy voice network and sells BroadWorks based UCaaS platforms through more than 1,500 resellers and MSPs across the UK, Germany, Spain, and the Netherlands, posting £645.8 million in revenue and 89% recurring gross profit last year. The deal capped a four month bidding war against Providence Equity Partners and an Oakley Capital and Giacom consortium, with Providence briefly teaming up with Epiris before walking away. Closing is expected in the first half of 2027, pending shareholder and antitrust approval across six jurisdictions. Epiris says going private frees up money for product development, commercial execution, and AI adoption. Analysts are calling it the largest UK telecoms deal of the year.
Dale's read

Fifteen hundred resellers and MSPs now report up to a platform under new ownership, and PE owners typically want faster returns than public shareholders tolerated. Expect Gamma's channel terms, roadmap, and pricing to get reviewed within the first year of private ownership, not the fifth. A four month bidding war also says private equity still sees real value in recurring UCaaS style revenue even while public markets keep discounting it, a gap worth remembering next time a comms platform's public valuation looks cheap.

Microsoft Just Opened the Enterprise Licensing Vault to Every MSP

Channel Dive · Sep 2, 2026
Microsoft is shifting its FY27 licensing strategy from three year Enterprise Agreements to a Cloud Solution Provider model built around seat growth and AI workload adoption, rather than treating existing installations as a stable annuity. That change knocks down a barrier that used to keep large enterprise licensing deals inside a small circle of Licensing Solution Providers with dedicated pricing desks. New platforms like Onyx, which closed a multi million dollar round from Triple Point in July, are automating the licensing analysis, opportunity scoring, and deal structuring that used to require that specialist expertise. Onyx co-founder John Dusett argues the shift does not remove expertise from the market, it spreads it around. Partners now have to evolve from processing transactions to managing growth, attaching security, governance, and implementation services around the license itself to capture value that used to sit with the LSP.
Dale's read

Licensing was the one line item locked behind a specialist relationship most partners couldn't reach. That lock is coming off, which means a wider field can now bundle Microsoft licensing into the same conversation as connectivity and UCaaS. It also means the margin that used to sit with an LSP is up for grabs by whoever moves first on attaching real services around it. Reselling the license won't be the differentiator, what gets wrapped around it will be.

Cisco Finds a Pile of Critical Bugs in Its Own Carrier Gear

The Register · Sep 4, 2026
A Cisco led security review turned up at least six critical to high severity flaws in IOS XR, the operating system that runs carrier grade routers, plus a separate unauthenticated remote root bug on ten Nexus 9000 switch models. Two CVEs scored a maximum 9.8. Patches are out for IOS XR; the Nexus flaw has no fix yet, only an access list workaround blocking two TCP ports.
Dale's read

No active exploitation reported yet, but a 9.8 sitting in gear that routes carrier traffic is exactly the kind of thing a security conscious prospect asks about directly. Know the patch status of anything running IOS XR or those ten Nexus models before they do.

Salesforce Just Handed Its Front Door to Anthropic

No Jitter · Sep 1, 2026
Salesforce and Anthropic launched Claudeforce, putting Claude in front of Salesforce's CRM data and workflows as the primary way users interact with the platform. Analyst Dave Michels compares the move to IBM outsourcing DOS to Microsoft in 1981, a decision that let Microsoft capture the software layer's value while IBM's hardware got commoditized underneath it. Salesforce is betting the trade works out better for it this time around.
Dale's read

If the AI layer becomes the interface reps actually touch every day, the CRM underneath starts looking like plumbing rather than the product. Any pitch built on "our platform has native AI built in" needs a sharper answer than that now that the CRM giant itself is outsourcing the front door.

OpenAI's Newest Model Can Find and Use Zero Days on Its Own

UC Today · Sep 2, 2026
OpenAI's Astra is the first model to cross the company's own "critical" cybersecurity threshold, hitting 100% on ExploitBench and independently finding and using two previously unknown vulnerabilities, including a sandbox escape. OpenAI paired the jump with tighter guardrails, refusing 91.5% of disallowed cyber requests versus 59% for its predecessor, and is limiting advanced access to a small tester group for now.
Dale's read

A model that can chain its own exploits is a preview of what eventually shows up in vishing and toll fraud tooling, not just a research headline. The guardrails holding today are the reassuring part; the capability underneath them is the part worth tracking once someone else strips them off.
