A Federal Judge Just Let the Otter.ai Privacy Case Move Forward

UC Today · Sep 7, 2026
A federal judge has allowed privacy claims against Otter.ai to proceed past a motion to dismiss, keeping alive a class action over how the AI notetaker records, retains, and reuses meeting audio. Plaintiffs in In re Otter.AI Privacy Litigation allege the company created voiceprints without proper consent and reused recorded conversations to train its own models, invoking the federal Electronic Communications Privacy Act plus California and Illinois privacy statutes, Illinois' biometric law among them. One case cites a venture capital meeting where the bot kept recording for two hours after the call ended and emailed the transcript to every participant, sensitive business discussion included. Judge Eumi K. Lee has not ruled on the underlying claims, only that they can proceed, but possible remedies include forced deletion of stored recordings and voiceprints. Otter.ai is one of the most common AI notetakers dropped into sales calls and pipeline reviews without a second thought.
Dale's read

Every AI notetaker on a sales call is quietly building a biometric and conversational data trail, and this case is the first real test of who owns it and what happens when someone objects. If a prospect asks whether transcripts get used for model training, know the vendor's answer before they do. Illinois' biometric law carries real teeth and the remedies on the table reportedly include forced deletion, not just a settlement check, so treat any AI notetaker's data policy as a live diligence item, not fine print.

X · @RobertFreundLaw, privacy attorney tracking the case
Update: Today, the court denied much of Otter's motion to dismiss. The plaintiffs have plausibly stated a claim for violations of the federal ECPA, and California's and Illinois's privacy laws.

Nokia Guts Bell Labs Research Staff by Nearly Half, Bets Big on Nvidia

Light Reading · Sep 8, 2026
Nokia plans to cut Bell Labs research headcount from 681 to 408 by the end of 2027, a 46% reduction, while shrinking dedicated research centers from nineteen to six. Annual research spending drops from roughly $250 million to about $100 million, or 0.4% of revenue, the lowest level in the lab's hundred year history. Nokia frames the move as renewal, redirecting resources toward GPU based AI-RAN development built around Nvidia's platform. Marcus Weldon, who ran Bell Labs from 2013 to 2021 and later served as Nokia's CTO, called the restructuring a top down dismissal of the lab's value, arguing the company is ignoring decades of profitable, slow to commercialize research in favor of a single vendor relationship. He warned Nokia now has "an over-dependence on Nvidia" and is putting "all its eggs in one basket."
Dale's read

A network vendor betting its core R&D budget on one GPU supplier is a supply chain story before it's a research story. If Nokia gear sits anywhere in a prospect's infrastructure roadmap, that dependency is now a fair question to raise in an executive conversation about vendor risk and roadmap continuity. It also shows where vendor money is actually going: not toward the decade out research that produced past breakthroughs, but toward whatever ships fastest against Nvidia's AI-RAN timeline.

Zoom's New AI Agent Triages Your Inbox While You're Out

UC Today · Sep 2, 2026
Zoom is rolling out Out of Office Workflows built on its ZoomMate agent: a "While I'm Out" canvas to hand off projects before leaving, a "Welcome Back" summary of meetings, docs, and chat on return, and a missed-message digest that triages action items into Do Today, Delegate, Defer, and Done. Zoom cites research claiming 78% of workers would disconnect more fully if AI reliably caught them up.
Dale's read

This has nothing to do with call quality or seat price, and that's the point. Zoom is competing on AI workflow polish now, not phone features, which means the next UCaaS bake off increasingly gets decided by what happens around the call, not during it.

UJET's Warning to Contact Centers Rushing AI: Slow Down

CX Today · Sep 8, 2026
UJET's Chief Customer Officer Kristin King says contact centers are deploying AI under board and competitive pressure without defining what problem it solves. Fragmented data across CRM, conversation, and quality systems undermines results, and frontline agents excluded from rollout decisions quietly work around the tools instead of adopting them. Her line: "Don't confuse speed with progress."
Dale's read

Every deal cycle right now has a prospect asking what AI is baked in. This is the counter argument worth having ready: a rushed AI rollout on fragmented data creates more escalations, not fewer, and that beats a feature list if the platform's own data foundation is actually solid.
