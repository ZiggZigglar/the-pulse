CrowdStrike Puts a Price on What Its Security Line Is Worth to Partners

Channel Dive · Aug 21, 2026
CrowdStrike expanded Project QuiltWorks, the cybersecurity coalition it launched four months ago with Accenture, EY, IBM and OpenAI, down into small and midmarket accounts, recruiting distributors Arrow Electronics and Pax8 and a wider MSP bench to carry it there. The original coalition paired CrowdStrike's AI-driven vulnerability tooling with big systems integrators; this expansion hands the same stack to MSPs serving businesses too small to run their own security team. VP Dan Danielli put a number on the pitch: "every dollar of CrowdStrike sold creates a little over $7 of partner services opportunity" across onboarding, monitoring and incident response. The math is the recruiting tool, not the technology. MSPs get a name-brand security line to bundle without building one, and CrowdStrike gets distribution into a segment it can't sell into directly.
Dale's read

Seven dollars of services for every one dollar of product is the kind of number a distributor puts in a partner deck, and it works because it's concrete. If your own security add-on story is "we partner with a vendor" and nothing else, that's the bar competitors are now selling against. Ask your own security partner what their services multiplier actually is, because "we have a partnership" isn't a number a prospect can compare.

Avaya Gets Its Third CEO in Four Years, Right as Its Cloud Bet Comes Due

No Jitter · Aug 24, 2026
Avaya named Jeff Clarke CEO in early August, its third chief executive in four years, moving Patrick Dennis to executive chair. The change lands as the company's cloud transition hits its real test: Infinity, its cloud UC and contact center platform, is already shipping, and Nexus, the next phase, is due in Q4 2026. Analyst Terra Higginson of Info-Tech Research Group frames the open question bluntly, whether Avaya's base actually moves to public cloud at all, or whether data sovereignty concerns push more of it toward hybrid deployments that keep infrastructure on premises. That's the same question every legacy PBX vendor answers differently, and Avaya has now changed the person answering it three times since 2022.
Dale's read

Three CEOs in four years is not a company that's found its footing, whatever the roadmap slide says. An Avaya shop in your territory is an opening, not a reason to wait for Nexus to ship. The sovereignty question Higginson raises is real leverage too: a prospect nervous about public cloud has a built-in excuse to stall a full Avaya migration, and that hesitation is exactly where a hybrid or full-cloud alternative gets a hearing.

Verizon Hands Google's AI the Keys to Its Own Network

RCR Wireless · Aug 26, 2026
Verizon and Google Cloud expanded their AI partnership so Gemini now handles the majority of consumer calls and chats in some channels, with human agents getting AI research assistants mid-call. The more striking piece: AI agents get programmatic API access to network infrastructure for automated patching and configuration changes, no human in the loop.
Dale's read

An AI agent pushing config changes to live network infrastructure with no sign-off is the kind of detail that shows up in a security questionnaire eventually. If a prospect cites Verizon's AI-run network as a trust signal, ask what happens when the agent gets it wrong.
