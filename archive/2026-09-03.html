Epiris Takes Gamma Communications Private in a £1.08 Billion Deal

UC Today · Sep 1, 2026
Epiris is buying Gamma Communications for £1.08 billion enterprise value, a 53% premium over the pre-offer share price, after Providence Equity Partners walked away and an Oakley Capital and Giacom consortium came up short. Gamma isn't a brand most buyers hear on a demo call, it's underneath one: the company runs voice network infrastructure behind providers like O2 Daisy and supplies BroadWorks-based UCaaS to more than 1,500 resellers and MSPs across four European markets. The numbers behind the deal are healthy, not distressed: £645.8 million in 2025 revenue, £141.7 million adjusted EBITDA, and 89% of that recurring. CEO Andrew Belshaw's stated case for going private is that running a communications platform under public reporting pressure is "increasingly complex" and leaves no room to invest in AI and product execution. The deal still needs shareholder and regulatory sign-off across seven countries, with close expected in H1 2027.
Dale's read

Every reseller sitting on Gamma's BroadWorks stack just inherited a private equity owner with a product roadmap nobody's seen yet. PE ownership usually means one of two things: real investment in the platform, or a pruned SKU list ahead of the next sale. Either way, if a competitor's UCaaS runs on Gamma underneath, that ownership change is a fair question for a renewal conversation, not just channel gossip.

AT&T Sues Charter Over the Word "Fiber" in Its Bundle Marketing

Light Reading · Sep 1, 2026
AT&T filed suit in the Northern District of Texas accusing Charter of deceptive advertising for branding its Spectrum hybrid fiber coax broadband as "fiber-powered," arguing the label misleads buyers into thinking they're getting fiber to the premises when the last mile still runs over coax. This follows a March complaint AT&T lost at the National Advertising Division, though Charter did adjust some disclosures afterward. Charter's response is blunt: "The NAD has already rejected AT&T's complaints... we think the court here will do the same thing." AT&T counters with a CableLabs figure that roughly 99.96% of packet travel in hybrid networks still happens over fiber, which it says makes "fiber-powered" a stretch for what the last mile actually is. AT&T is meanwhile pushing its own fiber footprint to 60 million homes by 2030, and Charter just closed its merger with Cox.
Dale's read

This is a fight AT&T is having in court, but the marketing question lands on every rep selling against a cable bundle: what does a prospect actually think "fiber-powered" means on a Spectrum business quote? If the answer is fiber to the building, that gap is worth raising before the incumbent has to explain it. Win or lose, the label just picked up a lot more scrutiny.

Optimum Locks In Five More Years of T-Mobile's Network

Telecompetitor · Sep 1, 2026
Optimum extended its wholesale mobile deal with T-Mobile another five years, adding 5G Standalone access, wider device support and better roaming on top of a mobile base already past 700,000 lines. Optimum calls it advancing its "broadband-mobile convergence strategy," the same playbook Comcast and Charter are running with their own MVNO deals.

Cable's mobile bundle keeps getting cheaper to run because it's rented, not owned, and every renewal like this buys the incumbent more years of a single-bill pitch. If your quote can't answer "why not just bundle mobile with my internet," that gap isn't closing on its own.

Telarus Data: 84% of IT Buyers Are Ready to Walk at Renewal

Channel Dive · Sep 1, 2026
Telarus's 2026-27 Tech Trends report, built on a Redpoint Insights survey of 503 midmarket and enterprise buyers, found 84% open to switching vendors at renewal and 87% who saw renewal costs rise this year. Constellation Research's Ray Wang put the mechanism plainly: "the new customers are getting the discounts," while existing accounts absorb the increases.
Dale's read

Loyalty discounting has it backwards on this beat, and the data just quantified how badly. A renewal call that leads with price protection instead of a new-logo-style offer hands a competitor the exact opening this report describes.

Tech Advisors Draft Their Own Ethics Code Ahead of an FCC Rulemaking

Channel Dive · Aug 31, 2026
TCSP is rolling out a certification program and code of ethics for technology advisors in Q4, after a 30-participant pilot, following an FCC crackdown on improper commissions tied to a rural healthcare program. Advisors aren't typically classified as USAC consultants but got swept into the scrutiny anyway. TCSP president Bill Power's logic: "If you've got a program, you at least have an argument when some higher power comes along."
Dale's read

Self-regulation shows up right before a rulemaking, not after, and that timing is the tell. A partner who can point to a certification and a written ethics code has an answer ready when a customer asks about commissions. One who can't is explaining after the fact.

Barracuda Stops Forcing Partners to Pick MSP or Reseller

Channel Dive · Aug 27, 2026

Gartner: AI Spending Is Eating the Rest of the Service Budget

CX Today · Sep 2, 2026
A Gartner survey of 199 service and support leaders found AI spending up 38% while total service budgets grew just 2%, meaning the AI line is funded by cuts elsewhere, not new money. Gartner's warning is blunt: with budgets flat, every AI dollar is visible to a CFO, and excitement alone no longer justifies it.
Dale's read

A flat budget with a 38% AI carve-out means somewhere else took the cut, and a prospect's finance team already knows it. Coming in with a measurable ROI case beats a capability demo now, because the demo doesn't answer what got defunded to pay for the pilot.
