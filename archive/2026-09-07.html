Nvidia Writes a $13 Billion Check for the Internet's AI Model Library

Broadband Breakfast · Sep 6, 2026
Nvidia confirmed it will pay $13 billion for Hugging Face, the open source repository used by 18 million developers to share more than 3 million models, 500,000 datasets, and a million applications across 200,000-plus companies. A billion of that is earmarked for employee retention, a tell that Nvidia is buying people as much as infrastructure. CEO Jensen Huang framed the deal as backing open source over letting a handful of proprietary labs, OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta among them, set the terms for how enterprises access AI. The stock moved about 2% on the news, a modest reaction for a deal this size, arriving days after Nvidia posted a $59.69 billion quarterly profit. West Monroe's chief AI officer called it a hedge against how AI evolves, which is another way of saying nobody, including Nvidia, is confident which model architecture wins yet.
Dale's read

Every CCaaS platform pitching agentic AI is built on somebody's model, and that somebody just got a lot more concentrated. When a prospect asks who is actually running the AI inside a contact center deal, "an open model hosted wherever Nvidia now controls" is a different answer than it was last week. Expect vendor due diligence questions to get sharper, not softer, from here.

Verint's New Numbers Say Contact Center AI Is Working, Mostly

CX Today · Sep 3, 2026
Verint's State of Contact Center AI 2026 report has the numbers sales teams love to quote: 87% of contact center leaders plan to increase AI spending, 62% now call the contact center a value center instead of a cost center, and 94% say AI tools improved human agent performance, with gains reported in both training and coaching. The report also surfaces the part that does not make it into the deck. The average contact center runs AI from three or more vendors, one in five runs five or more, and that sprawl fragments the customer data AI needs to actually work. On the buyer side, 64% of consumers say they have noticed AI in customer service, and only 62% of those found the experience positive, meaning a meaningful chunk of the market has already had a bad one.
Dale's read

The spending intent numbers are the ones vendors put on a slide. The vendor sprawl number is the one worth bringing into a discovery call, because a prospect running AI across five disconnected platforms cannot actually prove the ROI they are being sold on. Lead with consolidation, not another point solution, and the fragmented data stat becomes your opener instead of theirs.

iQor Says Its AI Platform Drives Revenue. The Math Doesn't Quite Add Up

CX Today · Sep 6, 2026
iQor's Insights iQ platform claims strong results: a retail client saw more than 30% of 1.4 million calls flagged automatable and 25% lower AI-handled contact costs, while a home services client reported a 43% jump in sales conversion and over $1 million in added revenue. A closer read finds iQor citing conflicting cost savings figures, 25% versus 8%, for what looks like the same retail case, with no published data on repeat contacts or full cost to resolution.
Dale's read

Vendor case studies read best when nobody checks the footnotes. Before a prospect leans on numbers like these to justify a platform switch, ask for the reconciled figures, not the slide with the biggest percentage on it.

The Gen AI ROI Numbers Contact Centers Are Actually Reporting

No Jitter · Aug 31, 2026
A No Jitter roundup of contact center survey data puts real numbers behind the AI ROI conversation: 90% of respondents report positive ROI overall, 65% call their AI initiatives successful, and 54% say benefits outweigh costs. Roughly seven in ten agents report saving time on after call work, and Priceline says automated call summarization cut 50 seconds off the average interaction.
Dale's read

Ninety percent reporting positive ROI is the stat that will not survive contact with a skeptical CFO. The 50 seconds Priceline shaved off after call work is smaller and more boring, which is exactly why it works better in a real conversation.
