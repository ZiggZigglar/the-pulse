Zoom guides down for Q2, Webex just had its best quarter in seven years

UC Today · Aug 18, 2026

Zoom told investors to expect Q2 revenue of $1.265 to $1.270 billion, a 4.1% year-over-year gain at the midpoint and a step down from the 5.5% growth CEO Eric Yuan called one of the company's best stretches in years. Cisco did not wait to make the contrast: Webex just closed its best quarter in seven years, with the Collaboration segment up 12%. RingCentral posted 5.9% growth in its latest quarter, also ahead of Zoom's own guidance. Zoom is still leaning on AI Companion, whose paid seats grew 184% year over year, but that adoption curve has not turned into matching revenue yet. Three vendors on the same buyer's shortlist just posted three different stories about where growth in video and calling actually lives right now, and it is not where Zoom is pointing Wall Street.

Dale's read

This is a live one for any deal where Zoom is the incumbent up for renewal. Webex's number gives Cisco shops a real growth story instead of a legacy label, and RingCentral's beat undercuts the idea that UCaaS growth has stalled industry-wide. It is Zoom specifically that is cooling. Flag the AI Companion gap too: seat growth without matching revenue makes a nice slide, not a renewal case.

Otter.ai loses its bid to dismiss the core claims over recording your meetings

UC Today · Aug 18, 2026

A federal judge in California refused to throw out the core claims in a class action against Otter.ai, the meeting transcription tool riding along in plenty of Zoom, Teams, and Google Meet calls. Judge Eumi K. Lee let federal wiretap claims, California privacy claims, and Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act claims move forward, rejecting Otter's argument that it is just a service provider passing data through. The court found enough evidence that Otter retained and used conversation data on its own account, including for training its models, to treat it as a possible third-party interceptor instead. Some Computer Fraud and Abuse Act claims got dismissed, and plaintiffs have 14 days to amend what is left. The BIPA piece is the one that stings: statutory damages run up to $5,000 per intentional violation, and voiceprint capture is what triggered it.

Dale's read

Every AI notetaker riding along on a sales call now has a live legal test case attached to it. If a prospect asks what happens to the voiceprint and transcript data their meeting tool captures, "the vendor trains on it" is not a safe answer anymore, at least not in Illinois. Worth knowing before pitching a bundled AI meeting assistant as a frictionless add-on.

Copper sunset keeps accelerating, and the FCC keeps saying yes

Broadband Breakfast · Aug 21, 2026

AT&T now has FCC approval to discontinue legacy service across more than 30% of its roughly 4,600 copper wire centers, effective late this year. Verizon wants to drop copper across millions of passings in 17 states, Fidium across hundreds of thousands of locations in eight, both citing subscriber counts as low as 4%. AT&T is also asking the FCC to preempt California's stricter carrier-of-last-resort rules, over state and consumer advocate objections.

Dale's read

Copper sunset timelines keep compressing faster than most legacy-line customers realize. Anyone still quoting a POTS-to-SIP migration on a leisurely runway should update that pitch. In a lot of these footprints the runway is now measured in months.

Gartner: only one in four AI customer service deployments actually pays off

No Jitter · Aug 18, 2026

Gartner looked at 432 AI customer service deployments and found a quarter delivering positive ROI, a quarter delivering negative returns, 42% with a payoff their own leaders could not quantify, and 11% breaking even. None of that has slowed spending. More than three-quarters of leaders surveyed plan to increase AI investment in 2026 regardless of whether last year's investment paid off.

Dale's read

Handy math for the next time a competitor's pitch leans on "AI-powered" as the whole argument. Ask what the ROI number actually is. Most of the time, per Gartner, there is not a good answer yet.
