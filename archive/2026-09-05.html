TPx Erases $1.1 Billion in Debt and Points the Savings at Partner Tools

Channel Dive · Sep 4, 2026
A federal bankruptcy court approved TPx's Chapter 11 reorganization plan on September 2, closing out a restructuring that started with a June 28 filing and moved through with what the judge called minimal revisions. The managed network services provider walks away having wiped out roughly $1.1 billion in debt, which CEO Shaun Andrews called "a real number" that "frees up a lot of capacity to grow." That capacity is going to the channel side first: faster quoting tools, an API tie-in with Connectbase for automated partner onboarding, a Salesforce integration to cut manual steps, and a signed SentinelOne deal for managed endpoint security. Andrews frames the goal plainly as making TPx "the easiest MSP for the channel to work with." Judge Alfredo Perez called the plan "the most value maximizing transaction available" to creditors.
Dale's read

A debt-free MSP with fresh money earmarked for quoting speed and partner APIs is a sharper competitor at the table, not a distressed one to route around. If a deal involves a managed services layer next to a UCaaS pitch, assume TPx shows up faster and easier to work with than it did a year ago. The bankruptcy is over. The buildout it funds is just starting.

Grain Management Merges Two Century-Old Carriers Into a 28,000-Mile Fiber Platform

Telecompetitor · Sep 4, 2026
Grain Management closed its combination of Ritter Communications and Great Plains Communications into a new entity called Rightfiber, backed by a $1.6 billion credit facility led by Fifth Third Bank for growth and further acquisitions. The combined platform covers more than 400 communities across 20 states on a 28,000-mile fiber network with over 300,000 fiber-to-the-home passings, putting it among the largest privately held fiber and telecom platforms in the country. CEO Heath Simpson pitched the merger as built on "shared values, exceptional people, and a commitment to investing in the communities we serve," with existing local support teams and customer relationships staying in place. The credit facility signals Rightfiber isn't done buying; it's positioned to keep rolling up smaller regional carriers in a fiber market where scale increasingly decides who can fund the next build.
Dale's read

Regional carriers keep consolidating for the same reason cable keeps bundling: standalone fiber operators can't fund builds at the pace private equity money can. A $1.6 billion credit line earmarked partly for more acquisitions means Rightfiber is a buyer, not just a merged asset, so expect its name to show up again within the year. Watch whether the combined footprint starts pushing business-grade products harder than either predecessor did alone.

FCC Floats a Robocall Scorecard, Cuts 14 Providers Off the Network

Telecompetitor · Sep 4, 2026
The FCC opened comment on a Robocall Scorecard meant to rate voice providers publicly on how well they fight illegal robocalls, an idea Chairman Brendan Carr says will "give consumers more information" and pressure laggards. Separately, the agency disconnected 14 voice providers for robocall violations and gave every other carrier two days to start blocking their traffic.
Dale's read

A public scorecard turns robocall compliance into a competitive selling point instead of a background cost center. If STIR/SHAKEN and traceback processes are already solid, that's a talking point now, not just a checkbox.

TDS Walks Away From Its Own Buyout Offer for Array

Broadband Breakfast · Sep 3, 2026
TDS, which already owns about 82% of Array, offered 0.86 TDS shares for every remaining Array share and then pulled the offer after the two sides couldn't agree on value. CEO Walter Carlson said the parties simply couldn't reach agreement "on the form of consideration and value." TDS will keep its minority stake, resume share buybacks with $523 million available, and instead focus on selling off Array's remaining spectrum, having already sent $1 billion in C-band licenses to AT&T and Verizon.
Dale's read

An all-stock buyout getting rejected by minority holders says spectrum is worth more held than sold into a merger right now. That's a data point on how the market is pricing infrastructure assets generally, and a reason not to assume every rumored carrier tie-up closes on the terms first floated.

Cato Rebuilds MSP Licensing Around Volume, Not Per-Seat Haggling

Channel Dive · Sep 2, 2026
Cato Networks launched SMB FlexPool, letting MSPs buy a pool of licenses and bandwidth up front and self-provision them in seconds instead of negotiating each seat individually. A new dashboard gives partners visibility across the full customer lifecycle. Channel chief Karl Soderlund says the old model meant constant "back and forth" between vendor and MSP just to get a customer live; Cato is coming off 42% year-over-year revenue growth to $415 million.
Dale's read

Deployment time dropping from days to seconds is the kind of detail a partner actually notices during onboarding, not a marketing line. Pooled, volume-based licensing rewards MSPs who sell more, which is exactly the program design a comp-sensitive partner base responds to fast.

SonicWall's Max-Severity Bug Is Already Being Exploited

Channel Dive · Sep 3, 2026
Two flaws in SonicWall's SMA1000 series, one a maximum-severity 10.0 CVSS server-side request forgery and the other a high-severity command injection, are being chained together for remote code execution on internet-exposed appliances. SonicWall confirmed active exploitation and CISA added both to its known-exploited list, giving federal agencies until Saturday to mitigate. This is the second SMA1000 exploitation wave in under two months.
Dale's read

A second wave of active exploitation on the same appliance line in eight weeks is a pattern, not a one-off patch cycle. Any customer running exposed SonicWall SMA1000 gear needs an answer today, not at the next scheduled review, and that urgency is a legitimate reason to get on the phone.

A Voice-Specific AI Model Claims It Beats OpenAI on Speed and Price

No Jitter · Sep 4, 2026
Phonely launched Alma, a language model built specifically for voice AI agents rather than adapted from text-trained models, which CEO Will Bodewes says makes it 63% faster and 84% cheaper than OpenAI for the same task. Alma runs as four specialized models working together for questioning, extraction, verification and orchestration, on Phonely's own infrastructure rather than a shared cloud API, aimed at cutting the latency spikes that plague concurrent calls in busy contact centers.
Dale's read

Purpose-built beats general-purpose on latency and cost when the job is narrow enough, and answering calls at volume is about as narrow as AI use cases get. Worth a real bake-off before assuming the general-purpose model already in a stack is good enough for voice specifically.
