TPx cancels its bankruptcy auction after nobody wanted to buy it

Channel Dive · Aug 13, 2026

TPx Communications, an Austin-based managed IT, UCaaS, and cybersecurity provider, told the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas on August 10 that it was canceling the sale auction built into its Chapter 11 plan. Bids were due August 7, the auction was set for August 12, and nobody qualified. TPx filed for Chapter 11 on June 28 with a restructuring support agreement backed by holders of roughly 98 percent of its funded debt, aiming to shed part of a 1.1 billion dollar debt load through either a sale or a lender-led recapitalization if no buyer showed up. With the auction dead, TPx moves to option two: a new first-lien exit facility of about 54.1 million dollars plus up to 64.7 million in tranche A and 9.8 million in tranche B preferred equity for its lenders. TPx kept paying sales agent commissions throughout the case. Creditors have until August 26 to object, with confirmation expected September 2.

Dale's read

Nobody bid on TPx even at bankruptcy prices, and that is the real headline here, not the recapitalization mechanics. If a competitor's assets are not worth buying at a discount, the accounts sitting on that platform are worth a call regardless of what the reorg plan says about business as usual. Commission continuity through the case is a data point for agents deciding whether to keep quoting TPx paper or start routing net new deals elsewhere.

RingCentral's breach data just landed in Have I Been Pwned, 1.6 million records

The Register · Aug 14, 2026

RingCentral disclosed on July 28 that a social engineering campaign, attributed to the ShinyHunters extortion crew, had compromised systems holding customer data, though the company said its core calling platform kept running without disruption. ShinyHunters claimed roughly 623 gigabytes taken and, after RingCentral declined to pay, published a 280 gigabyte archive. The Register reported on August 14 that the leaked data, about 1.6 million records with names, emails, phone numbers, and physical addresses, had been loaded into Have I Been Pwned, with 44 percent of the addresses already appearing in prior breaches. The intrusion method fits ShinyHunters' usual playbook: help desk impersonation and OAuth or session token abuse to get around multi factor authentication, rather than a flaw in RingCentral's product itself.

Dale's read

The platform stayed up, so this is not a downtime story to weaponize against RingCentral, it is a support desk story. ShinyHunters got in by social engineering a help desk, the same attack surface every UCaaS vendor runs, including whoever you sell. Use it to ask your own vendor how their support team verifies identity before making account changes, then ask the same question in the next RingCentral competitive deal.

ScanSource buys MicroAge for 220.5 million, betting on distributor-as-MSP

Channel Dive · Aug 21, 2026

ScanSource, parent of master agent Intelisys, agreed August 21 to buy IT integrator and MSP MicroAge for 220.5 million dollars in cash. MicroAge serves about 2,400 customers with cloud, cybersecurity, data center, and AI consulting, plus partnerships spanning Microsoft, Dell, and CrowdStrike. The deal closes by the end of September, funded through ScanSource's credit facility.

Dale's read

A distributor buying the MSP business its own channel used to service is convergence with teeth. Watch whether Intelisys stays neutral once its parent runs a competing services arm in house.

Somebody is selling live access to a US business phone system on Telegram

Brinztech · Aug 17, 2026

Threat researchers found a Telegram seller on August 17 advertising administrative access to a US SIP telephony domain, including control of call routes, internal extensions, spoofed caller ID, and 25 concurrent call channels, compatible with common softphone dialers. Buyers could use it for toll fraud, vishing that spoofs a trusted internal number, or passive call interception.

Dale's read

This is not hypothetical risk to gesture at in a security slide, it is a live listing. If a prospect's SIP trunk sits behind a weak admin portal or no MFA, that is the specific, current reason to bundle managed security into the phone conversation instead of pitching it separately.

Salesforce opens Agentforce's plumbing so any AI tool can drive your CRM

SiliconANGLE · Aug 19, 2026

Salesforce expanded Headless 360 on August 19 with an MCP server architecture that lets AI agents in Agentforce, Claude, ChatGPT, and other tools discover and invoke Salesforce capabilities directly, aware of the underlying permissions and workflows rather than just raw endpoints. A companion Data 360 MCP Server exposes roughly 200 APIs so agents can act on customer data without manual tool stitching, with general availability targeted for this month.

Dale's read

This is the plumbing that eventually lets an agent update a deal stage or trigger a follow up on its own. If your forecasting tool sits on Salesforce, ask the vendor whether they are building on this standard or on something proprietary that risks getting orphaned.
