Charter and Cox close the merger, and Spectrum is now everywhere

Telecompetitor · Aug 20, 2026

Charter Communications completed its acquisition of Cox Communications and Liberty Broadband on August 20, closing a deal that had been in motion for months and creating the largest broadband and video provider in the country, operating in 45 states under the Spectrum brand. Cox Enterprises walked away with about 33.6 million Charter common units worth roughly 5 billion dollars, 6 billion in convertible preferred units, and 4 billion in cash, leaving it with about 26 percent of the combined company on a fully diluted basis and a board seat for Cox chairman Alex Taylor, now Charter's chairman too. Liberty Broadband shareholders got 0.236 Charter shares per share held. Cox brings about 12 billion in debt into the deal. Charter is sweetening the transition with a year of free Spectrum Mobile service for former Cox internet customers, same day tech dispatch, and a floor of 20 dollars an hour for frontline workers.

Dale's read

This is the cable bundle you have been losing deals to, now bigger and in more zip codes. Forty five states under one brand means fewer places where "we already have internet, TV, and phone from the cable guy" is not the first objection in the room. The free year of Spectrum Mobile is a loyalty hook, not a gift. It trains customers to think in single bills right when you need them thinking best of breed. Know your overlap map before the next Cox or Spectrum call.

T-Mobile physically cut a cable to kick Chinese hackers off its network

TechCrunch · Aug 19, 2026

T-Mobile's cybersecurity team caught Chinese state hackers inside its network in 2024 and cut them off before any meaningful data left the building, chief security officer Jeff Simon disclosed publicly on August 18. The Salt Typhoon campaign had already compromised AT&T, Verizon, Viasat, Charter, and Windstream, hunting for call records and metadata tied to government officials. T-Mobile traced anomalous traffic to a compromised router at another, unnamed telecom company connected to its network, a trusted third party link the attackers used to get in without touching T-Mobile's own perimeter. Simon and three colleagues drove to a data center near Bellevue, Washington, found the compromised gear, and severed its external connection with a pair of scissors rather than wait on a software fix. T-Mobile declined to comment further when TechCrunch asked for detail.

Dale's read

Nearly every major carrier your prospects route calls through has had a nation state actor inside it in the last two years. That is not a talking point you invent, it is public record now. Any competitor leaning on "our network is more secure" just lost that line, because Salt Typhoon did not spare anyone based on size or spend. The question worth asking in a deal review is not whether a carrier got hit, it is how fast they noticed.

Salesforce partners still aren't making money off Agentforce

The Register · Aug 21, 2026

A TD Cowen survey of Salesforce partners found zero of them reporting that Agentforce is driving new bookings, with only a third hitting sales targets, down from 43 percent in the prior survey. Eleven percent said interest is minimal, 56 percent expect a long road to maturity. Marc Benioff maintains Agentforce has passed 1 billion dollars in annual recurring revenue.
